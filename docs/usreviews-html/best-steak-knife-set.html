    Buying Guide: How to Choose

    Serrated vs. straight edge

    Serrated blades stay sharp for years and cut through crust easily, but they cannot be resharpened. Straight-edge knives from brands like Wusthof and Zwilling give a cleaner slice and can be honed for a lifetime, though they need occasional maintenance.

    Steel and full tang

    High-carbon German stainless steel holds a keen edge and resists staining. A full-tang blade, where the steel runs the length of the handle, adds balance and durability that cheaper stamped knives lack.

    Handle, care, and looks

    Choose a handle that feels secure and balanced in hand, and decide whether you want dishwasher-safe convenience or hand-wash care that protects a finer edge. A matched set also elevates how your table looks when guests sit down.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What is the best steak knife set?

    The Amorston set is our top overall pick for its sharp full-tang blades, comfortable handles, and strong value, while Henckels, Wusthof, and Zwilling are excellent trusted-brand options.

    Are serrated or straight-edge steak knives better?

    Serrated knives stay sharp for years and handle crusty cuts well but cannot be resharpened. Straight-edge knives give a cleaner slice and can be honed for life, so it comes down to convenience versus maintenance.

    Can steak knives go in the dishwasher?

    Some sets are labeled dishwasher safe, but hand-washing protects the edge and handles on finer German steel knives, keeping them sharp and looking new for longer.

    The Bottom Line

    For most tables, the Amorston set offers the best mix of sharpness, comfort, and value. If you prefer serrated blades that never need honing, the Bellemain set is a favorite, and for an heirloom straight edge you can pass down, the German steel of Wusthof or Zwilling is worth the investment.
