Ask three reviewers about the TCL TAB A1 Plus and you will get three different numbers. WIRED gave it 7 out of 10. Techaeris gave it 9.0 out of 10. PCMag gave it 2.5 out of 5 and called it Fair. That is not a contradiction so much as a map of where the disagreement sits: every one of those reviews praises the hardware and every one of them finds something wrong with what the hardware is asked to do. This review is an attempt to work out which of those criticisms should change your buying decision and which should not.

This is a single-product review, not a roundup. We base it on manufacturer specifications, published hands-on testing, and aggregated verified-buyer feedback rather than our own trials. Our full method is on the [Our Method](/our-method/) page, and every source we relied on is listed at the bottom. We did not perform hands-on testing of this product.

## What the A1 Plus actually is

The A1 Plus is the largest and most expensive tablet TCL sells in this range. It is a 12.2-inch Android 16 tablet built on a Snapdragon 4 Gen 2, with 6GB of physical RAM, 128GB of storage, a microSD slot, and an 8,000mAh battery. The body is a 6.7mm aluminum unibody weighing 542g, rated IP54 for dust and splash resistance. It is available in a blue finish.

What matters here is that this is not a tablet with a large screen and entry-level everything else. The display, the build, the battery, and the software are all at or above the level of the rest of TCL's range, and the price reflects that.

![TCL TAB A1 Plus display graphic showing the 12.2-inch 2.4K panel, NXTVISION enhancement, and 120Hz refresh rate](/images/reviews/tcl-tab-a1-plus/pick-1.jpg)

- **Display:** 12.2-inch 2.4K (2400 × 1600), 120Hz, 550 nits, 100% sRGB, 3:2 aspect ratio, DC dimming
- **Processor and memory:** Snapdragon 4 Gen 2 (4nm) with LPDDR5, 6GB RAM plus up to 10GB expansion (16GB total), Adreno 613 graphics
- **Storage:** 128GB internal, microSD up to 2TB
- **Operating system:** Android 16
- **Battery:** 8,000mAh, 20W fast charging
- **Build:** 6.7mm aluminum unibody, 1.19 lb (542g), IP54 dust and splash rating
- **Audio:** Quad stereo speakers with Sound Booster, rated by TCL at up to 200% volume boost
- **Cameras:** 5MP front (centered), 8MP rear
- **Rating:** 4.3 out of 5 stars from 481 global ratings
- **Our score:** 8.2/10

One clarification on that rating: the TAB A1 Plus shares a single Amazon rating pool with the smaller TAB A1, because Amazon groups the two models under one parent listing. The 4.3 stars and 481 ratings are the same numbers you will see on the A1, not an additional set of ratings.

## The display is the reason to buy this

Everything else about the A1 Plus is either adequate or, in a couple of specific cases, a compromise. The display is neither. This is a 12.2-inch 2.4K panel running at 120Hz, and WIRED measured it at 236 pixels per inch — dense enough that text reads like text rather than like a rendering of text.

The color figure is the one that matters most, and it is the one that would have been trimmed first in a cheaper design. 100% sRGB coverage means the reds, greens, and blues are accurate against the standard rather than approximated. Techaeris confirmed DC dimming, meaning the panel holds brightness steady rather than pulsing to control it — the detail that separates a screen you can read in a dark room from one you cannot. TCL also puts the display through TÜV Rheinland's hardware-level low blue light certification, which addresses the same concern from the other direction.

![TCL TAB A1 Plus performance graphic showing the Snapdragon 4 Gen 2 processor and LPDDR5 memory](/images/reviews/tcl-tab-a1-plus/pick-2.jpg)

The 3:2 aspect ratio is worth pausing on, because it is the most consequential difference from the other tablets in this range and it is not a spec sheet item. A 3:2 screen is closer to the proportions of a printed page, which makes it noticeably more comfortable for reading documents, textbooks, and long articles. The 16:10 screens in the rest of TCL's range are wider, which suits video better and books worse.

## Performance is where the published reviews split

The Snapdragon 4 Gen 2 is a 2023-vintage chip built on 4nm silicon, paired with Adreno 613 graphics and LPDDR5 memory. Every published review of this tablet acknowledges that it is not a fast processor. What they disagree about is how much that matters.

Techaeris rated the tablet 9.0 out of 10 and observed the Adreno 613 dropping frames on demanding 3D workloads. In other words, the tablet handles reading, video, browsing, and the writing tools without trouble, and struggles specifically with graphics-heavy games. WIRED reached the same conclusion from the other direction, calling it a good everyday tablet and explicitly not a gaming machine.

PCMag's review is where the tone changes. It rated the A1 Plus 2.5 out of 5 and named three weaknesses: below-average battery life, limited and slow performance, and outdated wireless radios. The performance point matches what the other two found. The battery point is harder to dismiss and worth taking seriously — a tablet with an 8,000mAh cell that still underperforms on battery life suggests the cell is not the limiting factor, and that the chipset or the software is.

![TCL TAB A1 Plus storage graphic showing 16GB of memory, 128GB storage, and expansion up to 2TB](/images/reviews/tcl-tab-a1-plus/pick-3.jpg)

We think the honest reading is that this is a fast-enough tablet for reading, writing, video, and browsing, and a slow tablet for games — with battery life that reviewers have found disappointing at the top end of the capacity range. If your workload is the first set, the processor is a non-issue and the battery criticism matters more than it appears to. If it is the second, this is the wrong tablet.

## The AI features are real, and they are the same ones across the range

TCL ships the A1 Plus with Gemini, Circle to Search, Writing Assist, Text Assist, and Smart Translator, and the tablet's own marketing treats them as headline features. In practice they are genuinely useful rather than decorative: Writing Assist drafts and refines text, Text Assist works across apps, Smart Translator handles live translation, and Circle to Search pulls information from whatever is on screen.

![TCL TAB A1 Plus graphic highlighting the built-in AI productivity features](/images/reviews/tcl-tab-a1-plus/pick-4.jpg)

The multitasking layer is more conventional and just as useful. Split-screen, floating windows, Extend Mode, and Mirror Mode cover the practical cases: taking notes during a lecture, keeping a meeting app visible while checking a document, and using the tablet as a second display for a computer. PCMag listed the AI suite among the tablet's genuine strengths, which is not a small thing in a budget product.

## Battery, storage, and the parts nobody mentions

The 8,000mAh cell with 20W charging is the strongest battery specification in TCL's tablet range, and both A1 models share it. As noted above, PCMag's experience of the endurance was below average. Storage is 128GB internally with a microSD slot that accepts cards up to 2TB, and a 256GB version of the tablet exists in the range.

![TCL TAB A1 Plus multitasking graphic showing split screen, floating windows, Extend Mode, and Mirror Mode](/images/reviews/tcl-tab-a1-plus/pick-5.jpg)

![TCL TAB A1 Plus charging graphic showing the 8000mAh battery and 20W fast charging](/images/reviews/tcl-tab-a1-plus/pick-6.jpg)

The details that never make it into a comparison table: the front camera is centered on the long edge, which is the correct placement for landscape video calls and a small sign that TCL thought about the use case. The screen is glossy rather than matte, so it collects fingerprints and reflects in a bright room. Wi-Fi 5 is the one specification we would want changed, and PCMag flagged it as outdated; it is fine for the 5GHz network most homes run, but it is the ceiling on transfer speeds when you move a lot of video around.

## What is in the box

The box contains the tablet, a USB-C cable, a quick guide, and a SIM ejector pin. No wall charger is included — you will need to supply a USB-C charger yourself, and you will want one that supports 20W if you intend to use the fast charging.

## Who should buy it

Buy the A1 Plus if you spend your time on this tablet reading, writing, and watching video. The display is a genuine upgrade on everything else in the range, the 3:2 shape is the right one for documents, and the 542g body and 8,000mAh battery make it a realistic second device rather than a desk-bound one. It is also the only tablet in this range with published hands-on reviews from three independent outlets, which means you are buying a device with a documented track record rather than a spec sheet.

Buy it if you are a parent buying for a student, because the combination of a large readable screen, an 8,000mAh battery that lasts a full day of classes, and the full Android software stack with AI writing tools covers the school day well.

## Who should skip it

Skip it if you want to play demanding 3D games — published testing found the Adreno 613 dropping frames, and no amount of RAM expansion changes the processor underneath. Skip it if you need current wireless standards for heavy file transfer, since Wi-Fi 5 is the limit. And skip it if the fingerprint-prone glossy screen is going to bother you every day; the matte-finished models in this range do not have that problem.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is the TCL TAB A1 Plus worth it?

For reading, writing, video, and browsing, yes — that is where the 2.4K 120Hz display and 3:2 proportions pay off, and where the older processor is not a constraint. If your use is games or large file transfers over the network, the value proposition is weaker and you should look at a faster chip or newer wireless hardware.

### How good is the display on the A1 Plus?

It is the best in TCL's range and one of the better ones at this price tier generally. It is 12.2 inches, 2.4K, 120Hz, covers 100% of sRGB, uses DC dimming rather than pulse-width modulation, and carries TÜV Rheinland's hardware-level low blue light certification. WIRED measured it at 236 pixels per inch.

### How long does the battery last?

The cell is 8,000mAh and it charges at up to 20W, which is the best specification in the range. Actual endurance is where reviewers disagree: PCMag rated battery life as below average despite the cell size. We would not treat 8,000mAh as a promise of all-day-plus use without verifying it yourself.

### Does it come with a charger?

No. The box holds the tablet, a USB-C cable, a quick guide, and a SIM ejector pin. You need to bring your own USB-C charger, and a 20W-capable one if you want the fast charging.

### Is the screen matte like the paper-display models?

No. The A1 Plus has a glossy display. TCL also sells variants of these models with a paper-like matte display, which changes the reading experience and adds a stylus to the box — but that is a different product, and this review covers the standard glossy-screen A1 Plus.

### Can I add a stylus or keyboard?

Both are sold separately for this model. The stylus TCL pairs with these tablets registers 4,096 levels of pressure, but it is not included with the A1 Plus.

### How much storage can I add?

The tablet has 128GB internally and a microSD slot that accepts cards up to 2TB, so the practical ceiling is far above what the built-in storage offers.

### How long will it receive software updates?

It launches on Android 16. WIRED reports the A1 Plus is slated to receive operating system updates only through Android 17, with security updates provided for at least two years after the first shipment of a given device model.

## The bottom line

The TCL TAB A1 Plus is the best-made tablet in this range and the one most likely to be mistaken for something costing considerably more. The display is the reason: 12.2 inches of 2.4K, 120Hz, full sRGB, and DC dimming, wrapped in an IP54 aluminum unibody with 8,000mAh of battery and storage that expands to 2TB. That is a genuinely well-specified machine, and WIRED's verdict that it does not feel cheap is fair.

The criticisms are real but narrow, and knowing which ones apply to you is the entire point. A 2023-vintage Snapdragon and Wi-Fi 5 are invisible to anyone reading, writing, and streaming, and decisive for anyone gaming or shuttling large files. The battery finding is the one we would weigh most heavily, because it cuts against the headline specification. If you can live with one older chip in exchange for the best screen TCL makes, this is the tablet to buy.
