## Buying guide: How to choose

### Tank size and runtime

For bedrooms, a 1–1.5 gallon tank is enough for overnight use. For living rooms, 2–3.5 gallons gives you 24+ hours of runtime. If you don't want to refill frequently, prioritize larger tanks.

### Cool vs warm mist

Cool-mist humidifiers (evaporative or ultrasonic) are safer around children and quieter. Warm-mist humidifiers feel more substantial in cold air and can help with congestion. Both work — the choice is preference.

### Noise

For bedrooms, prioritize ultrasonic or evaporative models with low-noise ratings. Some units have dedicated sleep modes that dim displays and reduce fan speed.

### Smart features

Wi-Fi and app control are useful if you want auto-mode based on ambient humidity or scheduling. Voice control via Alexa and Google is a nice extra but not essential.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are humidifiers worth it?
Yes, especially in dry climates or during winter heating season. They help with dry skin, nosebleeds, wood furniture, and static electricity.

### Cool mist or warm mist?
Both work. Cool-mist is safer around children and quieter. Warm-mist feels more substantial in cold air and can help with congestion. Choose based on preference.

### How often should I clean my humidifier?
Every 1–2 weeks during active use. Empty the tank, wipe down with a damp cloth, and refill with fresh water. Some units have dishwasher-safe tanks.

### Do humidifiers help with allergies?
Yes, particularly in dry air. Humidity between 40–60% helps keep nasal passages moist, which reduces irritation.

### What size humidifier do I need?
Match the humidifier's coverage to your room size. For 200 sq ft bedrooms, a 1–1.5 gallon tank is enough. For 500+ sq ft living rooms, look for 2–3.5 gallon tanks with appropriate coverage.

### Are smart humidifiers worth the extra cost?
If you want auto-mode and scheduling, yes. If you just need humidity, a manual unit works fine.
