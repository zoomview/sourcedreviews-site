## Buying Guide: What to Look For in a Keurig

### Reservoir size

Larger reservoir means fewer refills. K-Elite has 75oz; K-Classic has 48oz; K-Mini has just enough for one cup.

### Brew sizes

Most Keurigs offer 3–5 brew sizes. If you want travel mug sizes (16–20 oz), look for Keurigs that support them.

### Temperature control

K-Elite and above offer temperature control. Cheaper Keurigs brew at one fixed temperature.

### Pod compatibility

All Keurigs use K-Cup pods. Most also accept reusable K-Cup filters (where you add your own grounds).

### Features

Strong brew, multi-stream, iced coffee modes are all worth considering if you regularly drink those styles.

## FAQ

### Are Keurigs worth the price?

Yes — Keurigs are reliable, well-supported, and have the largest pod ecosystem. Reusable K-Cup filters make them economical.

### What's the difference between Keurig models?

Mainly features: reservoir size, brew sizes, temperature control, and specialty modes (strong brew, iced, multi-stream). All brew K-Cup pods at roughly the same quality.

### Can you use non-Keurig pods in a Keurig?

Yes — most generic K-Cup pods work. Nespresso pods don't fit Keurig.

### How long do Keurigs last?

Typically 3–5 years with daily use. Descaling regularly extends life.

## Bottom Line

The Keurig K-Elite is the best Keurig for most people. For value buyers, the K-Classic is the time-tested choice. For those who need both single-serve and carafe, the K-Duo is the most flexible.
