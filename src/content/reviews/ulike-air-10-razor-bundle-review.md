## Why a razor belongs in an IPL bundle

The manufacturer's argument for the razor is sound, and it is worth stating plainly because most people buying a hair removal device do not think about it.

IPL works by sending light at melanin in the hair shaft, and the light has to reach the follicle. Hair that is long enough to stand away from the skin catches the light and turns it into heat on the surface instead of letting it travel down to the root. It also simply gets in the way of a treatment window that has to sit flush against the skin. So the workflow the manufacturer describes — shave first, then treat — is not a marketing convenience, it is the correct order of operations.

Where the razor earns its place is the second half of the argument. A standard razor asks you to bend, twist and reach, and it does not do either of those things well on a lower back, between the shoulder blades, or around the inner thigh. A long-handle razor is the cheapest possible fix for that specific problem, and the listing's own lifestyle photograph, the one that opens this review, shows a woman working a long-handle razor along her lower leg with the Air 10 resting on the white ledge of the bathtub beside her.

Three things in that table deserve emphasis.

**The timelines are not consistent.** The standalone listing says visible results in four weeks. The Deluxe listing says visible salon-like results in two weeks, on the same hardware with the same 6.6 J/cm2 and the same 26 J burst. Those two claims cannot both describe this device accurately. The clinical literature is the tiebreaker: the studies that produced good results used six treatments at two-week intervals, which puts meaningful change closer to month two than to week two. Treat the two-week claim as the more optimistic marketing position.

**Only the Deluxe listing tells you what is in the box.** It is the only one of the three with a written itemised list: the handset, the pink leather storage case, safety glasses, a shaving razor and a power adapter. That is worth more than it sounds, because it means the Deluxe buyer is not left wondering whether a glasses case or a charger is coming.

## What the clinical evidence says about the whole approach

Because all three listings are the same hardware, the published research on home IPL applies equally here, and we have covered it in full in our [standalone Air 10 review](/reviews/ulike-air-10-review/). The short version is worth repeating for anyone choosing between these packages.

Home IPL genuinely works, but on a timeline measured in months. A 2010 study of 22 patients using a home IPL device six times at two-week intervals found 95% of them noticed a reduced hair count, with 78% overall hair reduction at one month and 72% at three months, and no serious adverse events. A 2015 follow-up on facial hair below the cheekbone found an 83.3% reduction at one month and 78.1% at three months with no adverse events, again over six biweekly treatments.

It is also slower than going to a clinic. A 2023 head-to-head study on the underarms recorded 85% and 88% hair reduction for a professional diode laser against 52% and 46.3% for a home device, with mild side effects in both groups.

And the satisfaction data is the most useful calibration. In a cross-sectional survey of home light-based hair removal devices, 39 users who had bought one rated their satisfaction at 6.07 out of 10, against 7.15 for clinic laser, a statistically significant gap. Only 4 of those 39 reported any side effect at all.

The safety picture supports buying from a brand that ships protective eyewear. A 2023 systematic review of ocular damage from light-based home hair removal devices found no reported cases of eye damage following exposure to a home device. It did find 20 patients with iris atrophy, anterior-chamber inflammation or retinal pigment epithelium damage after office treatments at fluences of 20 to 24 J/cm2, and 40% of them had been wearing no protective eyewear. The authors specifically recommend protective goggles, clearer packaging warnings and never defeating a device contact sensor.

## Who should buy it

Buy this bundle if you treat areas you cannot comfortably reach with a normal razor. The lower back, the inner thigh, the area behind the arm, the centre of a chest — those are exactly the places where people abandon a home IPL routine halfway through, and a long-handle razor is the simplest fix that exists.

Buy it if you have already read our [standalone Air 10 review](/reviews/ulike-air-10-review/) and decided the device is right for your skin and hair colour, and you simply want the prep tool included.

## Who should skip it

Skip it if you reach the areas you need easily. A standard razor and a mirror handle most routines, and paying more for the bundled version gets you a tool you will use twice.

Skip it if you are buying on rating count. 55 ratings is a small pool, and a 4.3-star average over 55 people is a much noisier signal than 4.4 stars over more than 3,500. Read the review count as a confidence rating on the star figure, not as a separate quality judgement.

Skip it if you want a complete, guaranteed kit. This listing does not publish an itemised box manifest. If knowing exactly what is in the box matters to you, the [Deluxe version](/reviews/ulike-air-10-deluxe-review/) publishes one.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is the Air 10 razor bundle the same device as the plain Air 10?
Yes. Both listings use the model number UI20 DB, with the same dual lamps, 0.6 square inch treatment window, 6.6 J/cm2 energy density, 26 J SHR burst, sapphire cooling window and four modes. The bundle adds a long-handle razor and is sold in different packaging.

### Do I need to shave before using an IPL device?
Yes, and this is the reason the razor is in the box. IPL light has to travel down the hair shaft to reach the follicle. Long hair standing away from the skin absorbs that light at the surface instead, and a hair-free surface is also what lets the treatment window sit flush and deliver its energy evenly. The manufacturer describes the razor as being for hard-to-reach areas so that skin is clean and ready before a session.

### Is 4.3 stars from 55 ratings a bad rating?
Not on its own, but it is a much weaker signal than 4.4 stars from more than 3,500 ratings on the standalone listing. Treat it as low confidence rather than as a problem with the product.

### Which Air 10 package should I buy?
Buy the standalone Air 10 if you can shave the areas you need easily and you want the simplest option. Buy the razor bundle if reaching those areas is the friction that stops you using a hair removal device at all. Buy the Deluxe if you want the complete giftable kit, with a written five-item box list including safety glasses, a power adapter and a storage case. We review the standalone device here, the bundle on this page, and the Deluxe kit separately.

### Can I use the Air 10 on my face?
This listing names face treatment among the areas the device is meant for, alongside legs, arms, underarms and the bikini line. One of the clinical studies we cite specifically studied home pulsed light for facial hair below the cheekbone, with good results and no adverse events, though an earlier study excluded the face entirely. Treat face use as newer and less well established than body use.

### Is it safe on tattooed or tanned skin?
The listing explicitly states that the device is not suitable for tattooed, over-tanned or light-sensitive skin. The manufacturer's own applicability chart goes further and limits the supported range to Very Fair through Olive to Light Brown skin tones with black through light brown hair. The SkinSensor will stop flashing over unsuitable skin, but it is not a substitute for reading the chart.

## The bottom line

This bundle is the Air 10 with a problem-solving accessory attached, and the accessory is genuinely the right one. Shaving before IPL is not optional, and the parts of the body that are hardest to shave are the parts people quit on. If the lower back is on your list of areas, this is the version to buy.

What you are paying extra for is a razor, though, and on the evidence of the listing itself that is the whole difference: same model number, same lamps, same energy figures, same window. The bundle also has the weakest evidence base of the three, with 55 ratings and no written box list, which is why it scores just under the standalone device rather than above it. The Deluxe package, with its published five-item manifest and its storage case, is the better gift and the better all-round buy, and we explain why in the [Air 10 Deluxe review](/reviews/ulike-air-10-deluxe-review/).

Choose this one for the razor. Choose the Deluxe for the box. Choose the plain Air 10 for the price.
