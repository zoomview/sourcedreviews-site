## Buying guide: How to choose

### Capacity and household size

For one or two people, a 0.9–1.0 liter kettle is plenty. For a family of four, 1.5–1.7 liters gives you enough for several cups.

### Temperature control

For tea drinkers, variable temperature is useful — green tea brews best at 175°F, black tea at 205°F. For pour-over enthusiasts, 1°F increments give you precise control over extraction.

### Gooseneck vs standard spout

A gooseneck spout gives you precise pour control for manual brewing. A standard spout is faster and easier for everyday boiling.

### Body material

Stainless steel is durable and looks clean. Glass looks elegant but gets warm during use. Plastic is cheapest but feels less premium.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are electric kettles worth it?
Yes, if you drink tea or pour-over coffee daily. They boil water in 3–4 minutes and shut off automatically.

### What size electric kettle should I buy?
For one or two people, 0.9–1.0 liter is plenty. For a family, 1.5–1.7 liters.

### Are gooseneck kettles only for pour-over?
Primarily yes. The gooseneck spout gives you precise pour control for manual brewing. For everyday boiling, a standard spout is faster.

### How do I clean an electric kettle?
Descale monthly with a vinegar-water solution. Empty and rinse after each use.

### Are more expensive electric kettles better?
For pour-over enthusiasts, variable-temperature kettles like the Stagg EKG Pro are worth the premium. For everyday boiling, budget picks like the Hamilton Beach work fine.

### What temperature should I use for pour-over coffee?
195°F to 205°F is the standard range. Lighter roasts often do better at the higher end.
