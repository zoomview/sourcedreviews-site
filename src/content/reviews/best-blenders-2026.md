## Buying guide: How to choose

### Motor power

For ice and frozen fruit, look for 1,000+ W. For soft fruits and protein shakes, 600+ W is fine. Lower-wattage blenders burn out faster.

### Blade quality

Stainless steel aircraft-grade blades last longer. Blunt safety blades (Blendtec) are easier to clean. Avoid plastic blades in higher-priced units.

### Capacity

A 64 oz pitcher is standard for family blending. For personal use, a NutriBullet-style 32 oz cup is sufficient. For batch cooking or entertaining, 72+ oz pitchers work well.

### Container material

Glass is heavier but doesn't stain. Plastic (Tritan) is lighter and more durable. Some blenders offer both.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are expensive blenders worth it?
For heavy use (daily smoothies, ice, nut butter), yes. Vitamix and Blendtec last 10+ years with regular use. For occasional use, mid-range works fine.

### What wattage blender do I need?
For smoothies and ice, 1,000+ W. For protein shakes and soft fruits, 600+ W. Lower-wattage blenders burn out faster on hard ingredients.

### Can blenders make hot soup?
Some high-end blenders (Vitamix) can heat soup via blade friction in 5–7 minutes. Most blenders cannot.

### What's the best blender for smoothies?
The Vitamix 5200 is the most-cited blender in published guides for smoothies. The Ninja Professional is the strongest mid-range pick.

### How do I clean a blender?
Add warm water and dish soap to the container, run on high for 30 seconds, and rinse. Some blenders have self-cleaning cycles.

### Are personal blenders worth it?
For single-serve smoothies, yes. NutriBullet Pro 900 is the strongest personal pick. For family blending, full-size units are better.
