TCL's tablet range is unusually coherent for a budget brand. Three models cover almost the entire market, and they differ less by generation than by screen size — which is the decision that shapes everything else. The TAB A1 Plus is a 12.2-inch 2.4K panel running at 120Hz, built on a Snapdragon 4 Gen 2 with IP54 protection. The TAB A1 is an 11-inch 2.1K screen at 90Hz, with the same 8,000mAh battery, the same IP54 rating, and a smaller, lighter body. The TAB 10 Gen 4 is a 10.1-inch FHD tablet at 395g, built for reading, video, and children.

That shared DNA is the reason to consider the brand in the first place. The two A1 models run the same Android 16 software stack with the same Gemini-assisted features, the same expandable storage ceiling, the same quad-speaker-with-Sound-Booster arrangement, and the same centered 5MP front camera for calls. The TAB 10 Gen 4 is the odd one out: an older Android 15 build with a MediaTek chip, a single pair of speakers, and no IP54 claim on the listing.

This roundup draws on independent published testing, manufacturer specifications, and aggregated verified-buyer feedback. Our full method is on the [Our Method](/our-method/) page, and every source we relied on is listed at the bottom. We did not perform hands-on testing of any product we recommend here.

One note on ratings before we get into the picks, because it is easy to misread: the TAB A1 and the TAB A1 Plus share a single Amazon rating pool, because Amazon groups them under one parent listing. The 4.3-star average and the 481 global ratings you see attached to either model are the same ratings, counted once. The TAB 10 Gen 4 has its own separate listing and its own separate 4.3-star average from 184 global ratings. We are not adding these numbers together anywhere in this article, and neither should you.

## The picks, reviewed

### Best overall: TCL TAB A1 Plus

![TCL TAB A1 Plus tablet with a colorful display and its blue aluminum back showing the dual camera module](/images/reviews/tcl-tablets/pick-1.jpg)

The A1 Plus is the best tablet TCL makes, and the reason is the panel. A 12.2-inch 2.4K display at 2400 by 1600 pixels runs at 120Hz, covers 100% of the sRGB color space, and uses DC dimming rather than the pulse-width modulation that makes some budget screens uncomfortable at low brightness. TCL also puts the display through TÜV Rheinland's hardware-level low blue light certification. In a category where the display is usually the first thing cut, this is the opposite: TCL spent the money here.

The rest of the hardware follows the same logic. A Snapdragon 4 Gen 2 built on 4nm silicon with LPDDR5 memory and 6GB of physical RAM (plus up to 10GB of RAM expansion, 16GB in total) drives Android 16. Storage is 128GB with a microSD slot that accepts cards up to 2TB. The body is a 6.7mm aluminum unibody weighing 542g, rated IP54 against dust and splashes. The 8,000mAh battery takes 20W charging. Four stereo speakers handle the audio, with TCL's Sound Booster claiming to lift output by as much as 200%.

![TCL TAB A1 Plus display graphic showing the 12.2-inch 2.4K panel, NXTVISION enhancement, and 120Hz refresh rate](/images/reviews/tcl-tablets/a1plus-display.jpg)

What we hold against it is specific. The Snapdragon 4 Gen 2 is a 2023-vintage part, and published testing found it drops frames on demanding 3D graphics. PCMag's review rated the tablet 2.5 out of 5, calling out below-average battery life and outdated wireless radios alongside the good screen and useful AI tools. WIRED gave it 7 out of 10, praising the build while noting it is not a gaming machine. Techaeris gave it 9.0 out of 10, praising the panel, the metal build, and finding no bloatware. The three verdicts disagree because they weight different things — and on the axis that matters most to us, the display, the panel wins.

- **Processor and memory:** Snapdragon 4 Gen 2 (4nm) with LPDDR5, 6GB RAM plus up to 10GB expansion, Adreno 613 graphics

### Best value: TCL TAB A1

![TCL TAB A1 tablet showing a cream and gold abstract swirl wallpaper in front of its blue metal back with the circular dual camera module](/images/reviews/tcl-tablets/pick-2.jpg)

The A1 is the one to buy if you want the A1 Plus's build quality and battery at a step down in size, and it might be the one to buy even if you want the bigger tablet. It shares the A1 Plus's aluminum unibody, its IP54 rating, its 8,000mAh cell, its 20W charging, its quad speakers, its Gemini feature set, and its 2TB storage ceiling. What changes is the display and the chip.

The display is an 11-inch 2.1K panel at 2112 by 1320 pixels in a 16:10 shape, running at an adaptive 90Hz. TCL's listing advertises 650 nits of peak brightness — but The Gadgeteer, which compared the standard A1 against the paper-display NxTPAPER version of the same model, reports TCL rates the standard A1 at 500 nits typical. Read 650 as a headline peak rather than a number you will see every day. The 90Hz refresh rate is the more meaningful figure for scrolling and for the split-screen multitasking the software supports.

Underneath sits a MediaTek Helio G100, a 6nm octa-core chip paired with TCL's NXTURBO hardware-software optimization. It is a more modest piece of silicon than the Snapdragon in the A1 Plus, and Notebookcheck's launch coverage of the A1 and the A1 NxTPAPER described the pair as affordable 11-inch tablets built around a 90Hz panel and the 8,000mAh battery rather than around performance. If the A1 Plus is the better tablet and the A1 is the smarter purchase, this is why.

![TCL TAB A1 graphic showing the 11-inch 2.1K display, 650 nits, metal unibody at 430g and 6.2mm, and the 8000mAh battery](/images/reviews/tcl-tablets/a1-display.jpg)

### Best for kids and first tablets: TCL TAB 10 Gen 4

![TCL TAB 10 Gen 4 tablet angled in three-quarter view showing its purple 10:00 clock screen](/images/reviews/tcl-tablets/pick-3.jpg)

The Tab 10 Gen 4 is the lightest tablet in this roundup by a wide margin, at 395g, and it is the one built explicitly around what a child needs: a screen for reading and video, a camera for calls with family, a headphone jack, and software that lets a parent filter what is on it. Google Family Link and the Kids Space launcher are on the device, along with an Eye Comfort mode for longer reading sessions. The 10.1-inch IPS panel runs at 1920 by 1200 in 16:10 with TCL's NXTVISION picture enhancement, and the dual speakers carry the same up-to-200% Sound Booster claim as the bigger models.

The hardware underneath is more modest and the gaps in the published specification are worth knowing before you buy. The listing does not name a refresh rate for this model, and TCL's own product page does not publish one either. Storage tops out at 1TB on the microSD slot rather than the 2TB the A1 models allow, physical RAM is 4GB with an additional 8GB of RAM expansion on top, and it ships with Android 15 rather than the Android 16 found on both A1 tablets. The MediaTek MT8786V octa-core is the least powerful chip here, and TCL positions NXTURBO as much for power consumption and heat reduction as for raw speed. There is no IP54 rating on the listing.

The 6,000mAh battery with up to 18W charging is a smaller cell and a lower ceiling than the A1 models' 8,000mAh, and the charger is not included. Charging is over USB-C, which is the right choice, and the 3.5mm headphone jack means a child is not sharing Bluetooth headphones with a sibling.

## How to pick a TCL tablet

### Start with screen size, because it decides the rest

These three models are 10.1, 11, and 12.2 inches, and the jump from one to the next changes the physical character of the device more than any other spec. The TAB 10 Gen 4 at 395g is genuinely portable. The TAB A1 at 430g sits in the middle. The A1 Plus at 542g has crossed into the territory where a case or a shoulder bag stops being optional.

Resolution scales with size, but not perfectly: the TAB 10 Gen 4 runs 1920 by 1200, the A1 runs 2112 by 1320, and the A1 Plus runs 2400 by 1600. All three land in a sensible pixel density range for their size, so the practical difference is how much you can fit on screen, not how sharp the pixels are.

### Aspect ratio tells you what the tablet is for

This one is easy to miss. The A1 Plus uses a 3:2 ratio, which is closer to a sheet of paper and closer to a traditional reading experience. The A1 and the TAB 10 Gen 4 use 16:10, which is wider and better suited to video. If you are buying for reading — textbooks, documents, long articles — the 3:2 panel on the A1 Plus is the more comfortable surface, and that is one of the strongest reasons to pick it.

### Refresh rate and dimming behavior matter more than resolution

The A1 Plus runs at 120Hz, the A1 at an adaptive 90Hz, and the TAB 10 Gen 4 does not publish a figure at all. The difference is visible in scrolling and in the split-screen multitasking that all three support. Separately, the A1 Plus uses DC dimming, which means the screen does not pulse to control brightness — worth confirming for yourself if you are sensitive to it, since the other two do not make that claim.

### Processor generation sets the ceiling on everything else

The A1 Plus runs a Snapdragon 4 Gen 2 from 2023 with Adreno 613 graphics; the A1 runs a 6nm MediaTek Helio G100; the TAB 10 Gen 4 runs a MediaTek MT8786V. All three are entry-level silicon, and all three will handle reading, video, browsing, and the writing tools fine. None of them is a gaming tablet, and published testing confirmed the Snapdragon drops frames on demanding 3D. If your workload is media and documents, none of this matters. If it is games, look elsewhere.

### Battery and charging are nearly identical across the line — except at the bottom

Both A1 models carry 8,000mAh and 20W charging, which is the strongest pairing here. The TAB 10 Gen 4 drops to 6,000mAh and 18W. None of the three includes a wall charger in the box, so budget for one. PCMag's A1 Plus review is worth reading on this model specifically: it rated the tablet's battery life as below average despite the 8,000mAh cell, which is a useful reminder that capacity is a ceiling rather than a promise.

### Software and the AI features are the same on both A1 models

Android 16 with Gemini, Circle to Search, Writing Assist, Text Assist, and Smart Translator, plus split-screen, floating windows, and an Extend Mode that turns the tablet into a second screen for a computer. The TAB 10 Gen 4 runs Android 15 and swaps the AI suite for parental controls, which is the correct trade for a child's tablet.

### Stylus and keyboard are extras on every model

None of the three includes a stylus in the box — with the partial exception of the separate NxTPAPER edition of the A1, which bundles a T-Pen 2. Keyboards and cases are sold separately across the range, and PCMag's back-to-school coverage specifically recommends adding a third-party shockproof silicone case for younger children.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which TCL tablet is the best overall?

The TAB A1 Plus. It has the best display in the range — 12.2 inches, 2.4K, 120Hz, full sRGB coverage, and DC dimming — plus an aluminum unibody, an IP54 rating, 8,000mAh of battery, and 2TB of expandable storage. Its one significant weakness is that the processor is an older design, which shows up in demanding games rather than in reading, video, or writing.

### Is the TCL TAB A1 better value than the A1 Plus?

Often, yes. The A1 shares the A1 Plus's build, battery, IP54 rating, software, and 2TB storage ceiling, and adds a lighter 430g body and an adaptive 90Hz screen, while giving up some resolution and a better chip. Choose it when you want the A1 Plus's qualities in a smaller device, or when you spend most of your time reading rather than watching video.

### Does any TCL tablet come with a stylus?

The standard TAB A1 and TAB A1 Plus do not. The T-Pen 2, which registers 4,096 levels of pressure, is bundled with the separate NxTPAPER edition of the A1 and sold on its own for the standard models. The TAB 10 Gen 4 has no stylus support in the box either.

### Can I expand the storage on these tablets?

Yes, all three have a microSD slot. The A1 and A1 Plus accept cards up to 2TB, and the TAB 10 Gen 4 up to 1TB. All three ship with 128GB of internal storage, so the card is an addition rather than a replacement.

### Are these tablets good for kids?

The TAB 10 Gen 4 is the one built for it, with Google Family Link, the Kids Space launcher, Eye Comfort mode, face unlock, and a 3.5mm headphone jack for sharing. At 395g it is also the lightest of the three. The A1 models run the full adult software stack including AI writing tools, so parental controls are possible but they are not the organizing principle of the device.

### Do the tablets come with a charger?

No. All three list a USB-C cable in the box, and the TAB 10 Gen 4 listing says explicitly that the charger is sold separately. The TAB A1's listing notes the charging block is not included and points to the product safety sheet for a recommended charger.

### How long will these tablets receive software updates?

The A1 and A1 Plus both launch on Android 16. WIRED reports the A1 Plus is slated to receive OS updates only through Android 17, with security updates provided for at least two years after the first shipment of a given device model — which is the kind of detail to check against the specific configuration you are buying. The TAB 10 Gen 4 ships on Android 15.

## The bottom line

TCL has built a range where each model is defensible for a different person, and none of them is a trap. Buy the TAB A1 Plus if you want the best screen and the strongest build in the group, and you will have to accept an older processor to get there. Buy the TAB A1 if you want most of that quality in a lighter body at a step down in size, which is the value pick of the three. Buy the TAB 10 Gen 4 if the tablet is for a child or a first-time user, and you will get the lightest machine, the parental controls, and a headphone jack, in exchange for a slower chip and a smaller battery.

Our favorite of the three remains the A1 Plus, for one reason above all others: it is the only one whose display does not feel like the part that got cut.
