Oil of oregano supplements exist because the herb is a concentrated source of carvacrol, and this one is unusual in that it pairs the oregano oil with black seed oil rather than selling either alone. The Resilia pack ships as two pouches of 60 softgels — 120 in total — and the manufacturer presents it as a straightforward twice-a-day softgel rather than the dropper bottle of liquid oregano oil that most competitors sell.

That combination makes this a reasonable thing to consider if you have been looking at oregano oil and want the capsule format. It also puts this product squarely in a category where label literacy matters more than marketing, because "how much of what" is the only question the front of the pouch does not answer.

We base this review on the back-label Supplement Facts panel of the exact pouch sold in this listing, the manufacturer's own product graphics, and the Amazon listing record for this ASIN. We did not perform hands-on testing of this product.

## An honest note on the evidence behind this review

We could not find a published, independently verifiable clinical monograph covering oil of oregano and black seed oil as a combined supplement that we were able to read directly. That is a meaningful gap, and it is the reason this review does not repeat the immune or digestive claims printed on the front of the pouch.

What we can stand behind is narrower and more concrete: the exact milligram amounts of each active compound in the Supplement Facts panel, the serving size and servings per container, the capsule shell material, the carrier oil, the declared manufacturing origin, and the manufacturer-defined directions for use. Every specification in this review comes from that panel.

We also could not extract individual buyer review text from the listing, so we are not going to quote buyers or characterize a consensus of opinion. The only buyer data we can report is the aggregate Amazon rating shown on the listing at the time of writing: 4.2 out of 5 stars across 18,041 global ratings.

One more thing worth being explicit about: the statements on the front of this pouch — "supports digestive wellness," "immune function support," "provides antioxidant support" — carry the standard FDA disclaimer that they have not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. We are describing them as manufacturer claims, not as established effects.

## What this supplement actually is

- **Serving size:** 2 softgels
- **Servings per container:** 30 per pouch; the pack contains 2 pouches (60 servings, 120 softgels)
- **Oil of Oregano:** 300 mg per serving — (*Origanum minutiflorum*), standardized to 165 mg of carvacrol, as a 20:1 extract equivalent to 6,000 mg of oregano herb
- **Black Seed Oil:** 200 mg per serving — (*Nigella sativa*), cold-pressed, containing naturally occurring thymoquinone (TQ)
- **Capsule shell:** gelatin, glycerin, purified water
- **Carrier:** soybean oil
- **Formulated without:** Gluten, Dairy, GMOs, Artificial Flavors
- **Declared origin:** the back label states "Made in China with globally sourced ingredients" and carries a "Third-Party Tested" statement and a GMP Certified Practice mark
- **Lot and dates on the pouch we examined:** LOT 2026051615, MFG 05/16/2026, EXP 05/15/2028
- **Rating:** 4.2 out of 5 stars from 18,041 global ratings

Two numbers in that list do the heavy lifting. The 165 mg of carvacrol is the standardized figure — the manufacturer is telling you how much of the compound the oregano oil actually delivers, rather than just the weight of the herb powder. The 20:1 figure with the 6,000 mg herb equivalent explains where that number comes from: the extract is concentrated from a much larger quantity of dried herb. Both are manufacturer statements on the label, and neither tells you anything about whether the resulting capsule does what you want it to do.

## A soy-free claim the back label does not support

This is the part of the packaging that gave us pause, and we would rather tell you about it than smooth it over.

![RESILIA graphic headed Premium Ingredients showing an amber softgel surrounded by claims of non-GMO, soy-free and soy excluded, gluten-free and wheat free, dairy-free and milk free, and no tree nuts](/images/reviews/resilia-oil-of-oregano-softgels/pick-6.jpg)

The graphic above, from the manufacturer's own listing images, states "Soy-Free / Soy Excluded" as one of five premium-ingredient claims, alongside non-GMO, gluten-free, dairy-free and no-tree-nuts. The back label of the same pouch tells a different story.

![Back of the RESILIA Oil of Oregano pouch showing the Supplement Facts panel with 300 mg oil of oregano and 200 mg black seed oil per serving, and the other ingredients line reading soybean oil and gelatin softgel](/images/reviews/resilia-oil-of-oregano-softgels/pick-1.jpg)

The Supplement Facts panel lists the other ingredients as "Soybean Oil, Softgel (gelatin, glycerin, purified water)." Soybean oil is a soy derivative. The label's own "Formulated Without" row names four things — Gluten, Dairy, GMOs and Artificial Flavors — and soy is not among them.

We cannot reconcile those two statements from the packaging alone, and we are not going to guess at the manufacturer's reasoning for you. If a soy allergy or sensitivity is why you are reading the label closely, treat this as unresolved and contact the manufacturer before buying. The same caution applies to anyone avoiding pork or bovine products, since the capsule shell is gelatin and the label does not specify its animal source.

This is not a quality judgment about the formula. It is a note that the front of the package and the back of the package are telling you different things, which is exactly the kind of detail a supplement label should not have.

## The softgel format is the practical selling point

![RESILIA graphic headed Small, Smooth, Easy to Swallow showing a woman holding a softgel, with the softgel size stated as 15.4 x 8.5 mm and notes on smooth coating and no droppers or liquid measuring](/images/reviews/resilia-oil-of-oregano-softgels/pick-3.jpg)

Liquid oil of oregano is sold in dropper bottles and has two well-known problems: the measuring step is easy to get wrong, and the oil itself has a strong taste. A softgel moves both of those out of your hands. The stated size is 15.4 by 8.5 mm, which is a conventional adult softgel dimension, and the manufacturer describes a smooth coating specifically to avoid the "oregano oil on the tongue" problem.

Whether it avoids that problem in practice is a question we would need to test to answer, and we did not.

![RESILIA graphic headed Natural Variation showing two amber softgels side by side with an arrow between them, and text explaining that softgel color may naturally vary due to wild oregano sourcing and updated packaging while formula quality and consistency remain unchanged](/images/reviews/resilia-oil-of-oregano-softgels/pick-5.jpg)

The natural-variation graphic is a small thing that tells you something about how this product is made. Softgels derived from wild-harvested oregano will not be a uniform color from batch to batch, and a manufacturer that anticipates a question about it and explains it on the packaging is at least thinking about transparency. It also means a slightly darker or lighter softgel is not automatically a different product.

## What the manufacturer claims, and what we can verify

We can verify that carvacrol and thymoquinone are the two compounds this product is built around, because both are named on the Supplement Facts panel and in the marketing graphics. Carvacrol is the phenol that gives oregano oil its potency, and thymoquinone is the principal constituent of black seed oil that the manufacturer refers to as naturally occurring.

We cannot verify what either compound does for you. There is no dosage-specific clinical evidence for this product that we could read, the front-of-pouch claims are FDA-disclaimed structure/function statements, and the individual ingredients have not been studied in this 300 mg plus 200 mg combination in a way we could confirm.

The label's own precautions are more concrete and worth reading in full: pregnant or lactating women, people with medical conditions, and people taking medications — especially blood-thinning medications — are directed to consult a healthcare practitioner before use. Anyone on anticoagulants or antiplatelet drugs should raise a product containing this combination with a prescriber before starting it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much oregano oil is in each softgel?

Half a serving. The label sets the serving size at two softgels, and one two-softgel serving contains 300 mg of oil of oregano — standardized to 165 mg of carvacrol — plus 200 mg of black seed oil. So a single softgel carries 150 mg of oregano oil and 100 mg of black seed oil.

### Is this really 120 capsules?

Yes. The pack contains two pouches of 60 softgels each, for 120 total. Each pouch's Supplement Facts panel lists 30 servings at two softgels per serving, so the pair gives 60 servings. The manufacturer describes this as a 60-day supply at two softgels daily.

### Does this contain black seed oil?

It does, and it is a named part of the formula rather than a trace. The Supplement Facts panel lists 200 mg of black seed oil (*Nigella sativa*) per two-softgel serving, cold-pressed, containing naturally occurring thymoquinone.

### Is this product soy-free?

We cannot confirm that, and the packaging points in two directions. The marketing graphics state "Soy-Free / Soy Excluded," while the Supplement Facts panel lists soybean oil among the other ingredients. If this matters to you, ask the manufacturer directly and get the answer in writing before buying.

### Is the capsule vegetarian or vegan?

No. The other ingredients line reads "Soybean Oil, Softgel (gelatin, glycerin, purified water)." The shell is gelatin. The label does not specify which animal source the gelatin comes from.

### Where is it made?

The back label states "Made in China with globally sourced ingredients." That is the manufacturer's own label declaration — we did not independently verify the manufacturing site.

### How should it be taken?

The label's suggested use is two softgels daily, preferably with a meal, or as directed by a healthcare practitioner. Storage is a cool, dry place at 20–30°C (68–86°F).

### Does it help with immune or digestive health?

That is what the front of the pouch claims, with an FDA disclaimer attached, and we have no product-specific evidence to support or contradict it. The honest answer is that we cannot tell you, and neither can the packaging.

## The bottom line

The formulation on the label is specific and legible: 300 mg of oregano oil standardized to 165 mg of carvacrol, 200 mg of cold-pressed black seed oil, two softgels a day, 120 of them in the pack. If you want exactly that in capsule form, this product delivers it and tells you what is inside.

What stops us from recommending it outright is the packaging contradiction. A pouch that claims soy is excluded and then lists soybean oil as a carrier is asking you to choose which side of its own label to believe, and for a product marketed partly on ingredient purity that is not a small thing. The label also stops short of naming who third-party tested it.

Read the panel rather than the front, and if the soybean oil is a dealbreaker for you, ask before you order. Otherwise this is a competently made, clearly dosed capsule format of a combination that is at least fully disclosed.
