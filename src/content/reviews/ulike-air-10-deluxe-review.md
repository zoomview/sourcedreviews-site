## What the Deluxe actually adds over the standard Air 10

**A storage case that earns its space.** The pink leather case is the reason this package exists. It is not an afterthought and not a plastic shell: the listing photographs show the handset dropping into a lined compartment that holds the shape of the device, with a lid that closes flat. For a product that lives on a bathroom counter next to a sink, a case is the difference between a tidy shelf and a charging cradle with a cable trailing off it. It is also, plainly, a gift item.

![Ulike Air 10 Deluxe graphic showing 96% hair density reduction in just four weeks](/images/reviews/ulike-air-10-deluxe/pick-2.jpg)

**Safety glasses as a listed item rather than a photo prop.** The standard Air 10 listing shows safety glasses in the product photography but never writes down what is in the box. The Deluxe listing names them. That distinction matters more than it sounds: a 2023 systematic review of ocular damage from light-based home hair removal devices found no reported cases of eye damage following exposure to a home device, but it did find 20 patients with iris atrophy, anterior-chamber inflammation or retinal pigment epithelium damage after office treatments at fluences of 20 to 24 J/cm2, and 40% of those patients had been wearing no protective eyewear at all. The authors recommend protective goggles, clearer packaging warnings, and never defeating a device contact sensor. Having the glasses written into the manifest means you can confirm they arrived.

**An Auto Mode that fires every 0.25 seconds.** This is the one genuine functional addition. The listing describes an intelligent sensor that adapts energy to your skin tone, with the device flashing every 0.25 seconds in Auto Mode and an auto-glide function, for a full-body treatment in under ten minutes. The standard Air 10 listings mention neither an auto-glide function nor a flash interval. A quarter of a second between flashes is what makes a glide pass rather than a stop-and-click pass, and it is the feature most likely to be the reason someone finishes a session.

![Ulike Air 10 Deluxe graphic showing SHR mode with 26 J of energy, four flashes per session and double the power output of single-lamp devices](/images/reviews/ulike-air-10-deluxe/pick-3.jpg)

**A power adapter in the box.** Small, but one less thing to order — and the standard Air 10 listing never confirms one is included.

## Where the Deluxe's own claims need a caveat

The Deluxe listing is the most confident of the three, which is exactly why it deserves the closest reading.

**"Visible salon-like results in just 2 weeks."** The standard Air 10 listing says visible results in four weeks, and the razor bundle says as little as four weeks, on identical hardware. The clinical literature does not support two weeks. The two home-IPL studies with the clearest results used six treatments at two-week intervals, and the measured improvements were 78% and 83.3% at one month, easing to 72% and 78.1% at three months. Two weeks is a marketing position, not a clinical one.

**"96% hair density reduction in just 4 weeks."** The standard listing makes a nearly identical claim at 96.52%, measured on the bikini area and conditioned on using the device every two days for the first two weeks. Same number, different wording, different metric — hair density rather than hair count, and a specific measurement site. A bikini area is the easiest place to demonstrate a reduction, and it is not the area most people buy a hair removal device for.

**"Cools skin to 65 °F instantly."** The standard listing phrases the same feature as holding contact temperature below 65 °F after 30 minutes. Instantly is the stronger claim for a sapphire window. Either way, the honest read is that this describes the cooling surface rather than the light output, and neither version is an independent measurement.

**The 2X power output comparison.** The SHR graphic presents 26 J of energy per session, four flashes per session, and 2X the power output of single-lamp devices, with a footnote comparing against other products' single-lamp technology. That is a comparison against other home IPL devices, not against a clinic laser, and it should be read that way.

## The applicability chart and the safety test it insists on

The Deluxe listing includes Ulike's clearest version of the skin and hair colour chart, and it comes with an instruction worth quoting rather than paraphrasing: IPL requires contrast between the melanin in your hair and the melanin in your skin, it is not safe for all hair and skin types, and a skin safety test must be completed before treatment.

![Ulike Air 10 Deluxe applicability chart separating Good Fit from Not Recommend skin tones and hair colours](/images/reviews/ulike-air-10-deluxe/pick-4.jpg)

The Good Fit column covers Very Fair, Fair, Medium and Olive to Light Brown skin, with Black, Dark Brown, Brown and Light Brown hair. The Not Recommend column covers Brown and Dark Brown skin, with Red, Light Blonde and White to Grey hair. This is the same rule set the standard Air 10 listing publishes in a two-step format, and the consistency across the range is a point in the manufacturer's favour.

Two further notes. Skin safety testing is not a formality — the SkinSensor adjusts output based on the tone it reads, but the manufacturer still asks you to establish a baseline yourself. And the exclusion of red, blonde, white and grey hair is physics rather than caution: IPL needs melanin in the follicle to aim at.

## What the published evidence says about the device in this box

Because the hardware is identical to the standard Air 10, the full body of home-IPL research applies unchanged. We set it out in detail in our [Air 10 review](/reviews/ulike-air-10-review/), and the short version is this.

Home IPL works, on a timeline of months. The 2010 study of 22 patients using a home IPL device six times at two-week intervals found 95% noticed reduced hair count, with 78% overall hair reduction at one month and 72% at three months, and no serious adverse events. The 2015 follow-up on facial hair below the cheekbone found 83.3% reduction at one month and 78.1% at three months, again with no adverse events, over six biweekly treatments.

It is slower than a clinic. A 2023 head-to-head on the underarms recorded 85% and 88% hair reduction for a professional diode laser against 52% and 46.3% for a home device, with mild side effects in both groups.

Satisfaction lands near 6 out of 10. In a cross-sectional survey, 39 home-device users rated satisfaction at 6.07 out of 10 against 7.15 for clinic laser, a statistically significant gap, and only 4 of the 39 reported side effects at all.

The safety record supports buying the kit with glasses in it. The 2023 ocular-damage systematic review found no reported eye damage from home devices, but its recommendations on protective eyewear, packaging warnings and not defeating contact sensors are exactly what this package provides.

And on long-term risk, a review of more than 25 years of laser and IPL use concluded there is no evidence of a credible cancer risk, while noting that home-use devices now have parameters comparable to salon equipment.

## Who should buy it

Buy the Deluxe if you are buying this as a gift. The pink leather case, the written box manifest and the included glasses and adapter make it a present that looks like a present, and the recipient is not left guessing what is in the box.

Buy it if you want Auto Mode. A 0.25 second flash interval with auto-glide is the difference between a session you finish and a session you abandon, and it is the only functional difference between this package and the standard device.

Buy it if you have the shelf space. A 3.9 pound kit that lives in a proper case is tidier than a device with a cable trailing across the counter.

## Who should skip it

Skip it if you treat your own body and already own a razor, a case and a charger. You would be paying for accessories rather than capability, because the machine underneath is the same one.

Skip it if you are buying on the results claim. Two weeks is not what the research says, and the Deluxe listing is the one making that promise.

Skip it if you have red, blonde, white or grey hair, or if your skin is in the deepest tone range. The chart in this listing rules you out explicitly, and the hardware would not work for you at any setting.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What comes in the Ulike Air 10 Deluxe box?
The listing states it explicitly: the Air 10 Handset in Purple, a Deluxe Pink Leather Storage Case, a pair of Safety Glasses, a Shaving Razor and a Power Adapter. It is the only one of the three Air 10 listings that publishes this list, which is the main reason to buy this package over the others.

### Is the Air 10 Deluxe a different device from the Air 10?
No. Both carry the model number UI20 DB, with the same dual lamps, 0.6 square inch treatment window, 6.6 J/cm2 energy density, 26 J SHR burst, sapphire cooling window and four modes. The Deluxe adds the case, glasses, razor and adapter, plus an Auto Mode that flashes every 0.25 seconds with auto-glide.

### Does the Deluxe really work faster than the standard Air 10?
It claims visible results in two weeks where the standard listing says four. The clinical studies that produced good outcomes used six treatments at two-week intervals, so meaningful change lands closer to month two on either version. The one genuine speed difference is Auto Mode's flash interval, which affects how long a session takes, not how long results take.

### Is it worth buying the Deluxe over the plain Air 10?
If you want safety glasses, a storage case and a power adapter confirmed in writing, and you like the idea of a 0.25 second auto-glide flash, then yes. If you can shave comfortably yourself and do not care about the box, the standard Air 10 gives you identical hardware for less. We cover the standard device in detail in our [Air 10 review](/reviews/ulike-air-10-review/), and the razor bundle option in our [bundle review](/reviews/ulike-air-10-razor-bundle-review/).

### Are the before-and-after results on the listing real?
The listing's graphics carry individual-results disclaimers, which is the minimum a marketing image should do. The published research on home IPL reports measured hair-count reductions of 72% to 83% at one to three months over six biweekly treatments, which is in the same range as the claims, but on a bikini area and under a specific protocol rather than as a general promise.

### Is the pink leather case worth anything?
It is the main reason to choose this package. A home IPL device lives on a bathroom counter, and a fitted case with a flat-closing lid is what keeps that counter tidy. It is also the reason the kit photographs and presents as a gift.

## The bottom line

The Air 10 Deluxe is the best-presented Air 10, and the presentation is the product. Same handset, same lamps, same 26 J SHR burst, same sapphire cooling window — but a pink leather case, a written five-item box manifest, safety glasses and a power adapter that you know are coming, and an Auto Mode that flashes every quarter of a second so you can glide instead of stopping at every patch.

The claims need the usual haircut. Two weeks is not what the research shows; four weeks on the standard listing is closer, and the clinical picture runs to months of twice-weekly sessions. The 96% figure is a bikini-area measurement under a specific protocol. What you are really buying here is a better box around a machine that is already good.

If it is for you, or for someone you are buying for, this is the version to get. If you are buying for yourself and can handle a normal razor and a spot on the counter, the standard Air 10 gives you the same machine for less money — see our [Air 10 review](/reviews/ulike-air-10-review/) for that version.
