## Buying Guide: What to Look For

### Pod vs grounds

K-Cup-compatible brewers give you access to thousands of pod varieties. Grounds-only brewers are cheaper and produce less waste.

### Reservoir size

A larger reservoir means fewer refills. Solo drinkers can use a 40oz reservoir; small households need 50oz+.

### Brew sizes

Most brewers offer 4–12 oz sizes. If you want travel mugs (16–20 oz), look for brewers that support that.

### Hot + iced

Some single-serve brewers have dedicated iced coffee modes that brew at lower temperatures and concentrate the coffee to compensate for ice melt.

### Size

K-Mini and similar compact brewers are under 5 inches wide; standard Keurigs are 9–12 inches wide.

## FAQ

### Are single-serve coffee makers worth it?

If you drink one cup at a time and don't want to brew a whole pot, yes. If you regularly brew for 2+ people, a regular drip brewer is more economical.

### Keurig vs Nespresso: which is better?

Keurig has more pod variety and is more affordable. Nespresso makes better espresso but has higher pod costs.

### How much do K-Cups cost?

Generic K-Cups are around $0.30–0.50 per cup. Name-brand K-Cups are $0.50–0.80. Reusable K-Cup filters (where you add your own grounds) cost ~$0.10 per cup.

### Are reusable K-Cup filters good?

Yes — they let you use any ground coffee. Slightly less convenient than pods but dramatically cheaper and more sustainable.

## Bottom Line

The Keurig K-Elite is the best single-serve brewer for most people. If you want compact, the K-Mini is hard to beat. If you want both single-serve and carafe functionality, the K-Duo does both.
