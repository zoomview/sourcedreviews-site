## Buying guide: How to choose

### Capacity and household size

For one or two people, a 4-quart slow cooker like the Hamilton Beach is plenty. For a family of four, 6–7 quarts is the sweet spot. If you regularly batch-cook or entertain, an 8-quart model gives you headroom.

### Manual vs programmable

A manual slow cooker with three settings is cheaper and simpler. A programmable timer is genuinely useful if you cook during the day and want the cooker to switch to warm automatically. Most modern picks have programmable timers.

### Multi-cooker vs dedicated

A multi-cooker like the Instant Pot replaces several appliances. If you already own a pressure cooker or rice cooker, a dedicated slow cooker is simpler. If you have limited counter space, a multi-cooker consolidates.

### Cleaning and storage

Look for dishwasher-safe stoneware and lids. Some models have removable lids that clip onto the base for serving — a small but useful feature.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are slow cookers still worth it?
Yes. They are inexpensive, reliable, and produce consistently good results with minimal effort. Modern slow cookers are safer and more programmable than older models.

### What size slow cooker should I buy?
For one or two people, 4 quarts is plenty. For a family of four, 6–7 quarts is the sweet spot. For batch cooking or entertaining, 8 quarts.

### Can I leave a slow cooker on all day?
Yes, but most modern programmable slow cookers switch to warm automatically after the cook time. If yours is manual, use a kitchen timer or outlet timer to avoid overcooking.

### Are programmable slow cookers better than manual ones?
Programmable slow cookers are more convenient for daytime cooking. Manual models are cheaper and simpler. Both produce similar results.

### How do I clean a slow cooker?
Most stoneware and lids are dishwasher-safe. For stuck-on food, soak in warm soapy water overnight.

### Are more expensive slow cookers better?
Not always. The Crock-Pot 7-Quart outperforms several pricier models in our cross-source research. Premium picks like the Breville add probe-based precision that most people don't need.
