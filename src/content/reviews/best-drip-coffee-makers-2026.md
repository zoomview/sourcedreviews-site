## Buying Guide: What to Look For in a Drip Coffee Maker

### Brew temperature

The SCA-recommended range is 195–205°F. Any brewer that consistently hits this range will produce dramatically better coffee than one that doesn't.

### Carafe type

Thermal carafes keep coffee drinkable for 2+ hours. Glass carafes with warming plates can scorch coffee after 30–60 minutes.

### Brew time

The SCA recommends 4–8 minutes for a full pot. Faster is generally fine, slower is fine if extraction stays even.

### Programmability

If you want coffee ready when you wake up, look for a 24-hour programmable timer.

### Capacity

12-cup brewers handle most households. 10-cup is fine for 1–2 people. 14-cup is for offices or large families.

## FAQ

### Are expensive drip brewers worth it?

Up to a point. The jump from a $40 brewer to a $200 brewer is real (better temperature control, more even extraction). The jump from $200 to $400 is mostly build quality, not coffee.

### How often should I clean my drip coffee maker?

Every 1–2 months, run a descaling cycle (white vinegar + water, or a commercial descaler like Affresh).

### Glass vs thermal carafe — which is better?

Thermal if you often have leftover coffee. Glass if you drink the whole pot in 30 minutes.

### Do paper filters matter?

Yes — paper filters produce a cleaner cup but trap some oils. Permanent mesh filters let oils through but produce a slightly muddier cup. Both are valid.
