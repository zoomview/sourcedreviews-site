Most UPS units are boxes you put on the floor. This one goes on a wall.

The GOLDENMATE 1500VA/1000W is a flat, wall-mountable uninterruptible power supply with a metal housing, eight protected outlets, a colour LCD, USB-C and USB-A charging ports, a communication port for automatic NAS shutdown, and a built-in LED that turns on by itself when the power fails. It is a genuinely different proposition from the tower-style unit most people picture, and the feature list is unusual rather than padded.

Two things are worth flagging before anything else. This listing publishes a smaller body of buyer feedback than its sibling — 50 global ratings rather than thousands — so its 4.5-star aggregate rests on a much thinner base. And the listing's own product graphic and its own specification table give different dimensions for the same chassis.

## An honest note on the evidence behind this review

We did not perform hands-on testing. Nobody here has mounted this on a wall, cut power to it, or watched the emergency light come on in a dark room. Everything here comes from the manufacturer listing record and the product images published with it.

We quote no individual buyers. The aggregate rating on this listing at the time of writing is 4.5 out of 5 stars from 50 global ratings — a platform aggregate of buyer submissions, not our editorial score, and a small one. Amazon's generated summary of those reviews reports that buyers find the unit reliable, with one mentioning it kept their PC running without a single hiccup, and that they appreciate the wall-mounting capability and consider it a good unit. We report those themes as Amazon's summary of reviews, not as our findings.

Every manufacturer statement below is a manufacturer claim, attributed as such. Where the listing's documents contradict each other, we show the contradiction.

## The size, and where the two documents disagree

![GOLDENMATE graphic titled Color LCD Display, showing the dark grey 1500VA/1000W unit with its lit colour screen, dimension lines marked 10.03 inch, 13.54 inch and 3.54 inch, and a weight label of 13.89 lbs](/images/reviews/goldenmate-1500va-wall-mount-ups/pick-2.jpg)

This graphic is the manufacturer's dimensional drawing, and it is worth reading carefully against the specification table.

The graphic marks the unit at 10.03 inch, 13.54 inch and 3.54 inch, with a weight of 13.89 lbs. The specification table records 3.54"D x 9.84"W x 13"H, with an item weight of 13.9 pounds.

The weight agrees. The dimensions do not. The 3.54-inch depth matches. The other two figures do not: the table gives 9.84 inches wide by 13 inches high, while the graphic gives 13.54 inches wide by 10.03 inches high. Those are two different chassis sizes, and they cannot both describe the same product.

If you are cutting a shelf, mounting in a network closet or checking whether it clears a skirting board, measure the actual unit rather than trusting either number.

## The features that make this one different

![GOLDENMATE graphic titled Power Uninterrupted, Devices Protected, showing the dark grey wall-mounted unit beside a monitor on a desk, with icons for 1500VA/1000W, LiFePO4 Battery, 8 AC Outlets and Wall-Mounted](/images/reviews/goldenmate-1500va-wall-mount-ups/pick-1.jpg)

The wall mount is the structural idea. The manufacturer states the unit can be installed using the included screws, freeing up shelf space in network and IT closets while keeping the setup tidy. In a closet already carrying a NAS, a PoE switch and a router, the space this frees is the point.

The metal housing is a second departure from the norm. The listing records the enclosure material as metal, and the manufacturer describes the housing as durable.

And the electrical claims are the conventional ones for this class: pure sine wave output with an under-10 ms transfer time, and continuous voltage monitoring that the manufacturer says handles outages, surges and fluctuations for computers, NAS devices, monitors, home servers and network equipment.

## The communication port and automatic NAS shutdown

![GOLDENMATE graphic titled NAS-safe Auto Shutdown, showing a flow diagram of Power Failure leading to PSignal leading to Safe Shutdown, above a desk with a laptop, the wall-mounted UPS and a NAS enclosure](/images/reviews/goldenmate-1500va-wall-mount-ups/pick-5.jpg)

This is the feature that most distinguishes the unit from a generic backup box, and the graphic lays the sequence out plainly: power failure, then a signal, then a safe shutdown.

The manufacturer describes it as a USB cable connection to a Windows PC, Mac or NAS via the communication port, enabling automatic safe shutdown during outages to help prevent data loss, file corruption and system crashes. This is the one thing the 1000VA tower unit does not do, and for anyone running a NAS it is often the reason to choose a UPS at all.

## USB-C and USB-A charging

![GOLDENMATE graphic titled Power More Devices at Once, showing the wall-mounted UPS above a desk with a laptop, monitor and a phone, with USB-C and USB-A ports labelled on the left](/images/reviews/goldenmate-1500va-wall-mount-ups/pick-3.jpg)

The unit includes USB-C and USB-A ports with 10 W total output, for charging a phone, tablet, smartwatch or similar device. The manufacturer describes eight AC outlets offering both surge protection and battery backup alongside the USB ports.

One thing worth being clear about: the listing does not say the USB ports keep charging from the internal battery. The 10 W figure is what the manufacturer specifies for the ports; whether they stay alive on battery is not something the listing states.

## The built-in emergency light

![GOLDENMATE graphic titled Built-in Emergency Light, Auto On During Power Outage, Stay Safe in the Dark, showing the wall-mounted unit glowing above a desk in a dark room with a person working at a laptop](/images/reviews/goldenmate-1500va-wall-mount-ups/pick-4.jpg)

A built-in LED emergency light switches on automatically during a power outage, and the manufacturer's stated purpose is to give instant visibility to check connections, locate devices and move safely even in complete darkness. It turns off automatically once utility power is restored.

There is a specific instruction attached to it: set the LED light switch to AUTO to enable automatic operation, or to OFF if you do not want it. That switch setting is worth knowing before you rely on the feature — a unit sitting in the OFF position will not light up when you need it.

## The display, the buzzer, and the buttons

![GOLDENMATE graphic titled Ultra-quiet Operation, showing a woman working at a desk beside the wall-mounted UPS, with a sound scale marking 60dB convection, 55dB whisper and 50dB UPS, and a Mute Button callout](/images/reviews/goldenmate-1500va-wall-mount-ups/pick-7.jpg)

The LCD is a colour screen reporting real-time battery and power status. The manufacturer describes its behaviour precisely: it lights up automatically when the unit is powered on and turns off after 90 seconds of inactivity; pressing the display button briefly reactivates it, while holding the display button for three seconds keeps it continuously on.

When mains power goes abnormal and the unit switches to battery mode, a built-in buzzer sounds. Pressing the mute button silences it temporarily; holding it for three seconds disables it until the next restart.

The graphic shows a three-position noise scale: 60 dB for convection cooling, 55 dB for whisper and 50 dB in UPS mode. Those figures come from the manufacturer's graphic rather than from the specification table, so treat them as a published claim rather than a measurement.

## The battery

![GOLDENMATE graphic titled Lithium UPS Battery Backup, showing the dark grey unit with a translucent cutaway revealing a green battery pack, labelled 230Wh, 10 Year Lifespan and 3000+ Cycle life at 1500VA/1000W](/images/reviews/goldenmate-1500va-wall-mount-ups/pick-6.jpg)

The manufacturer describes an advanced lithium iron phosphate battery pack offering over 10 years of use and 3,000+ charge cycles, and claims that this beats traditional lead-acid battery backup and surge protector systems by saving up to 40% in long-term replacement costs. The graphic backs the same figures, with 230Wh printed on it.

Worth noting for anyone cross-shopping: the graphic gives 230Wh for this 1500VA unit, and the specification table gives 230.4Wh for the smaller 1000VA tower. The two units are published with the same battery capacity but different power ratings.

Also worth noting that this listing, unlike the 1000VA listing, names no specific safety standards in its specification table. The feature bullet refers to certifications for safety and industry compliance, "advanced chip technology" and double anomaly protection, but does not name UL1642, UL1973, UL1778, FCC or TUV the way the tower unit's table does. If a named standard on the box matters to you, ask.

## Two operating notes from the manufacturer

The first is the generator warning, and it is worth reading in full. Generator-supplied power may not meet the unit's input requirements for voltage, frequency, waveform or grounding, and may therefore not be recognised as valid AC input. In that case the unit stays in battery mode and the battery will not recharge.

The second is the input range: the unit supports 90–140 V AC input, designed for standard 110 V household power, and the manufacturer asks you to verify your outlet voltage before use.
