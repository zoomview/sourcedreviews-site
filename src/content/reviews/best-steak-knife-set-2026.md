## Buying Guide: What to Look For

### Construction type

Full-tang knives have a blade that runs through the entire handle, which makes them stronger and prevents handle loosening. Partial-tang or stamped knives are cheaper but less durable.

### Steel type

High-carbon stainless steel holds an edge better than regular stainless but requires more care. German steel (WÜSTHOF, Zwilling) is generally excellent. Japanese steel (VG-10, SG2) is even harder but more brittle.

### Serration

Aggressive serration cuts through tough meat easily but is harder to resharpen. Fine serration looks more refined but loses edge faster.

### Handle material

Wood handles are traditional but require care. Composite handles are dishwasher safe but less photogenic. Stainless handles are durable but can be slippery.

### Set size

Most sets include 4–8 knives. Match size to household and dining habits. 8-knife sets are good for larger families or entertaining.

## FAQ

### How long do steak knife sets last?

Premium sets (WÜSTHOF, Zwilling) can last 20+ years with care. Budget sets last 3–5 years before needing replacement.

### Are serrated or straight-edge steak knives better?

Serrated stay sharper longer (because the serrations create new edges). Straight-edge cuts cleaner but needs sharpening more often.

### Should I buy a knife block or set?

Sets with blocks are convenient but more expensive. Sets without blocks are cheaper if you already have storage.

### How do I care for steak knives?

Hand-wash and dry immediately. Store in a block or on a magnetic strip. Sharpen annually or as needed.
