## Buying Guide: What to Look For

### Filter type

Paper filters produce the cleanest cup, but trap some oils. Mesh filters are reusable but produce a slightly muddier cup. Cloth filters are traditional but require maintenance.

### Carafe material

Glass is the standard, looks beautiful, but is fragile. Stainless is more durable but conducts heat differently. Ceramic looks nice but is heavier.

### Size

Match size to household. A 2-cup pour-over is for solo use; a 6-8 cup Chemex is for couples or small groups.

### Learning curve

Chemex and V60 have a real learning curve. If you want pour-over quality with less effort, consider an automatic pour-over like the OXO.

## FAQ

### Is pour-over better than drip?

Pour-over gives you more control over extraction, which can produce a cleaner cup. But for most people, a good drip brewer produces nearly indistinguishable results.

### How long does pour-over take?

Manual pour-over takes 3–4 minutes per cup once you're practiced. The first few times will take longer.

### Do I need special coffee for pour-over?

You can use any coffee, but freshly ground beans make the biggest difference. A burr grinder (not blade) is essential.

### Are expensive pour-over filters worth it?

The Chemex bonded filters are noticeably better than generic. For V60, the genuine Hario filters are also slightly better than generic. The difference is small but noticeable.

## Bottom Line

The Chemex is the pour-over setup most experts point to. If you're a single-cup drinker, the Hario V60 is more practical. For hands-off pour-over, the OXO Brew is a solid automatic option.
